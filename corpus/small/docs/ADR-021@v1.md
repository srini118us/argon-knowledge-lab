# ADR-021: Retention for the order-events topic

## Summary

Retention for the `order-events` Kafka topic is set to **14 days**. This overrides the **7 day default** defined in POL-DR-02 **for `order-events` only**. All other topics keep the policy default.

## Context

POL-DR-02 (Disaster recovery and event retention) sets a default retention of 7 days for Kafka topics on the Orion platform. That default has served us well. It keeps storage predictable, limits the blast radius of accidentally retained sensitive data, and is long enough for nearly every consumer to recover from an outage by replaying from its last committed offset.

`order-events` turned out to be the exception.

Over the last several incident reviews, a recurring theme came up: **reporting backfills after incidents needed more than a week of events.** The pattern looked roughly like this:

1. Something goes wrong with reporting. Sometimes it is a consumer bug that silently mis-handles a field, sometimes a schema change that was not picked up, sometimes a stuck consumer nobody noticed over a long weekend.
2. The problem is detected some days later, usually when a business user notices that numbers look off.
3. By the time the fix is written, reviewed and deployed, the start of the bad window is close to, or already past, the 7 day retention boundary.
4. The team can no longer replay `order-events` from the beginning of the affected window, and has to reconstruct history from other sources. That reconstruction is slow, manual and error prone.

Engineers on the reporting side described the 7 day window as "just barely too short, every single time". Detection lag plus fix lag routinely consumed most of the week.

## Decision

- Set retention on the `order-events` topic to **14 days**.
- This is an explicit, documented exception to POL-DR-02. The 7 day default remains in force for every other topic.
- Any future request to extend retention on another topic must go through its own ADR. This record does not establish a general precedent that teams can raise retention by asking.

## Options considered

**Keep 7 days and improve detection.** We should improve detection regardless, and that work is tracked separately. But even with faster alerting, fixes take time to write and review, and we did not want recovery to depend on everything going right.

**Raise the global default.** Rejected. Most topics do not need it, and raising retention everywhere increases storage and widens exposure for data that has no reason to be kept longer. POL-DR-02's default is still the right answer for the general case.

**Archive order-events elsewhere and replay from the archive.** Technically workable, but it adds a second replay path with its own tooling, failure modes and access controls. For the size of the problem, a longer retention on one topic is simpler and easier to reason about.

**14 days on order-events only (chosen).** Doubles the replay window for the one topic where it has repeatedly mattered, with no change elsewhere.

## Consequences

**Positive**

- Reporting can replay a full two weeks of order history after an incident, which covers the detection plus fix window we have actually seen in practice.
- Incident recovery for reporting becomes a replay rather than a reconstruction project.

**Negative / things to watch**

- Storage for `order-events` grows roughly in proportion to the longer window. Platform should keep an eye on disk usage for this topic after the change.
- Data on this topic now lives longer. Producers should continue to avoid putting anything on `order-events` that does not need to be there.
- There is now one topic whose retention differs from the policy. Anyone reading POL-DR-02 in isolation will get the wrong answer for `order-events`. We will add a pointer to this ADR from the topic's configuration notes and from the reporting runbooks.

## Implementation notes

- Change the topic level retention setting on `order-events` through the usual configuration change process, not by hand on the cluster.
- Confirm after the change that the effective retention reads back as 14 days.
- Update consumer runbooks for reporting so the on-call engineer knows the replay window is two weeks for this topic and one week for everything else.
- No consumer code changes are required.
