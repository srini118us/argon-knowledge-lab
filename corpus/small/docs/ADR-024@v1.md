# ADR-024 Outbound limits for carrier-gateway

## Summary

carrier-gateway will enforce a hard cap on outbound traffic to SwiftShip and will protect itself with a circuit breaker. This ADR is written as a follow up to postmortem **PM-2025-014** (Carrier label outage), which asked for an ADR to cap outbound traffic to SwiftShip.

## Background

carrier-gateway is the component in the Orion platform that talks to SwiftShip, our carrier API vendor, for things like creating shipments and generating labels. It is the only place in Orion that holds SwiftShip credentials and knows the shape of the SwiftShip API, which is exactly why we want the protective logic to live there and nowhere else.

PM-2025-014 described what happens when carrier-gateway has no outbound discipline. A wave of failing calls triggered retries, the retries triggered more retries, and the resulting retry storm pushed our request rate above the SwiftShip rate limit, which was then 50 requests per second. Once we were over the limit, SwiftShip started rejecting calls, which produced more failures, which produced more retries. Labels stopped being generated and the outage lasted until the traffic was brought back under control by hand.

The postmortem produced two follow ups. The first was to reduce retries in runbook RB-CARRIER-01, which has been done and is tracked there. The second was this ADR. Retry tuning on its own only changes how fast a storm builds; it does not stop one. We need a ceiling that holds regardless of what the retry logic, the callers or the vendor are doing.

Since the incident, SwiftShip agreed **CT-SWIFT-2024 Amendment 1**, which raised the contracted rate limit to **120 requests per second**. That gives us more room than we had in August, but it does not remove the need for a cap. Without one, a large enough storm would simply hit the new limit instead of the old one.

## Decision

### Outbound rate cap

**carrier-gateway outbound traffic to SwiftShip is capped at 100 requests per second.**

The cap is enforced inside carrier-gateway, across all instances, and applies to every call that leaves carrier-gateway for SwiftShip, including retries. Retries do not get a separate budget. When the cap is reached, carrier-gateway holds or rejects further requests locally instead of sending them to SwiftShip.

**The cap deliberately stays below the contracted SwiftShip limit to leave headroom.** The contracted limit is 120 requests per second under Amendment 1. Running right up against a vendor limit means any measurement drift, clock skew between instances, or difference in how SwiftShip counts requests puts us over the line. The gap between our cap and the contract is there so that we hit our own limit, which we control and observe, long before we hit theirs.

### Circuit breaker

carrier-gateway wraps SwiftShip calls in a circuit breaker. **The circuit breaker opens after 5 consecutive failures.** While it is open, carrier-gateway stops sending requests to SwiftShip and fails fast locally. After a cool down, the breaker moves to a half open state and lets a trial request through. A success closes the breaker; a failure opens it again.

What counts as a failure: timeouts, connection errors, rate limit rejections from SwiftShip and server side errors from SwiftShip. Client side errors caused by bad request data do not count, because they say nothing about SwiftShip's health and would otherwise let one malformed shipment trip the breaker for everyone.

### Observability

Both mechanisms must be visible. carrier-gateway will emit metrics for:

- outbound request rate to SwiftShip, compared against the cap
- requests held or rejected locally because of the cap
- circuit breaker state changes
- consecutive failure count

Breaker state changes should page the on-call engineer who looks after carrier-gateway, and the dashboard used during carrier incidents should show the cap and the current rate side by side.

## Alternatives considered

**Rely on SwiftShip's own limit.** SwiftShip rejects traffic above the contracted rate anyway, so why add our own? Because that is the failure mode from PM-2025-014. Vendor rejections turn into errors on our side, errors turn into retries, and we end up in exactly the loop we are trying to avoid.

**Set the cap equal to the contracted limit.** Rejected for the headroom reasons above.

**Retry tuning only.** Already done via RB-CARRIER-01, and worth doing, but it is not a ceiling. A burst of genuine new traffic combined with modest retries can still exceed the vendor limit.

**Cap upstream instead of in carrier-gateway.** This would spread the logic across every service that needs carrier operations, and none of them can see the total rate. A single choke point in carrier-gateway is simpler and correct.

## Consequences

- During a SwiftShip slowdown or outage, carrier operations will fail fast instead of piling up. Upstream Orion work that depends on carrier operations must already cope with carrier-gateway failures; this ADR makes those failures quicker and more frequent during vendor trouble, not new.
- Under very high legitimate demand, some requests will be held or rejected by our own cap even though SwiftShip could have taken them. We accept that trade. If sustained demand approaches the cap, the right response is to revisit the cap and the contract together, not to remove the cap.
- The cap value is a configuration setting, but changing it is an architectural change and needs a new or amended ADR, along with a check against whatever SwiftShip contract terms apply at that time.
- The carrier incident runbook (RB-CARRIER-01) should reference the breaker and cap metrics so the on-call engineer can tell quickly whether a problem is ours, theirs, or our own protection doing its job.
